## EGI — Ethical Governance Intelligence
Physical AI Execution-Path Safety Control Standards

A Horizontal Safety-Control Framework for AI-Involved Physical Systems
Developer: Jeong-Pyo Na, Republic of Korea
Initial completion: August 2, 2026
Current revision: Revision 7 — November 12, 2026
Standards: 15 technical standards

1. Overview
EGI (Ethical Governance Intelligence) is a proposed horizontal safety-control framework designed to prevent unsafe physical execution in AI-involved systems.
EGI addresses the safety boundary between AI-generated execution requests and their realization as physical actions.
Its central principle is that an AI-generated command must not automatically constitute authorization for physical execution.
Instead, physical execution is subject to independent safety determination, explicit execution authorization, non-bypassable enforcement, and verification of actual intervention results.
The framework is intended for application across different categories of AI-involved physical systems, including humanoid robots, industrial robots, medical robots, care robots, and multi-robot systems.

2. Core Safety Architecture
The EGI framework establishes the following safety-control sequence:
AI-Involved Execution Request
↓
Independent Safety Determination
↓
Execution Authorization
↓
Non-Bypassable Execution-Path Enforcement
↓
Physical Safety Intervention
↓
Verification and Recording of Actual Results
The architecture requires safety decisions to be enforceable within the physical execution path.
Where immediate interruption could introduce additional hazards, the framework provides for controlled transitions toward a safer operating state.

3. EGI Industrial Safety Standards — 15 Standards
The EGI standard series consists of three foundational standards and twelve supplementary standards.
Foundational Standards
Standard 01 — Requirements
General safety requirements for AI-involved physical execution, including independent safety determination, execution authorization, execution-path enforcement, intervention, fault response, recovery, and traceability.
Standard 02 — Vocabulary
Terminology and definitions supporting consistent interpretation and application of the EGI safety-control framework.
Standard 03 — Test Methods
Verification methods, test procedures, and conformity assessment criteria for evaluating compliance with EGI requirements.

4. Technical Objectives
The EGI standards aim to establish common requirements for:
Functional and authority-based independence of safety control
Execution authorization based on defined invariant conditions
Prevention of unauthorized physical execution
Non-bypassable enforcement within execution paths
Physical intervention in response to hazardous conditions
Controlled safety-state transitions
Prevention of unintended or unauthorized re-execution
Fault detection and fail-safe response
Verification of actual intervention results
Traceability and conformity assessment
EGI is designed as a horizontal framework rather than a standard for a particular robot model, manufacturer, or AI algorithm.

5. Relationship to International Standardization
EGI is proposed as a common safety-control framework for AI-involved physical systems.
Its requirements are intended to complement relevant functional safety, machinery safety, robotics safety, and AI safety standards.
The EGI approach focuses specifically on the relationship between AI-involved execution requests, independent safety authorization, physical execution-path enforcement, and verification of safety interventions.
The EGI standards are currently independent technical drafts and have not been adopted as ISO, IEC, or Korean national standards.
